What to actually check on a second viewing

A first viewing is for falling in love with a place. A second viewing is for figuring out whether that feeling survives contact with reality — and it rarely gets the attention it deserves.

Go back at a different time of day than your first visit. Light, noise, and street parking all change.

Run every tap and flush every toilet. Open every window and door in the house, not just the ones the listing photos favored. Check mobile signal in the rooms you'll actually spend time in, not just the living room where the agent is standing.

If the property backs onto a road, street, or shared wall, visit at the time of day that road is busiest — usually that's a weekday commute window, not a quiet Sunday afternoon showing. None of this is about being difficult; it's about knowing what you're actually buying before the inspection period closes.
